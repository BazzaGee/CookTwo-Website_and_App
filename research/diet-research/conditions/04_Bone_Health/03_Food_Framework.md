# Bone Health Diet — Food Framework

## Daily targets

| Nutrient | Target |
|---|---|
| Calcium | 1,000–1,200 mg/day (split across meals, ≤500 mg per absorption window) |
| Vitamin D | 800–1,000 IU/day for adults 50+ (medical guidance if deficient) |
| Protein | 1.0–1.2 g/kg/day, ~25–35 g per main meal |
| Vitamin K | 90–120 µg/day from leafy greens |
| Magnesium | 310–420 mg/day from food |
| Sodium | ≤2,300 mg/day; ideally ≤1,500–2,000 mg |
| Caffeine | ≤400 mg/day |
| Alcohol | ≤1 drink/day for women, ≤2 for men; ideally less |
| Cola drinks | avoid |

## Calcium-rich foods (per serving)

| Food | Calcium |
|---|---|
| Plain yogurt, 200 g | ~250–300 mg |
| Milk, 250 ml | ~300 mg |
| Fortified plant milk, 250 ml | ~300 mg (check label) |
| Calcium-set tofu, 100 g | ~200–350 mg |
| Sardines with bones, 100 g | ~380 mg |
| Canned salmon with bones, 100 g | ~200 mg |
| Kale, cooked, 100 g | ~150 mg |
| Collard greens, cooked, 100 g | ~140 mg |
| Bok choy, cooked, 100 g | ~100 mg |
| Almonds, 30 g | ~75 mg |
| Tahini, 2 tbsp | ~130 mg |
| Sesame seeds, 2 tbsp | ~180 mg |
| Dried figs, 3 | ~80 mg |

## Plate structure

- **½ plate vegetables and fruit** — emphasise low-oxalate greens (kale, collards, bok choy, broccoli), plus peppers, tomatoes, carrots and fruit.
- **¼ plate protein** — fish (including sardines/salmon with bones), poultry, eggs, tofu, legumes, dairy.
- **¼ plate whole grains or starchy vegetables** — oats, brown rice, quinoa, barley, potatoes, sweet potatoes.
- **Calcium accent** — yogurt, fortified plant milk, tahini dressing, almonds or sesame at each meal.

## Choose freely

- Dairy: milk, yogurt, cheese (moderate due to sodium/saturated fat).
- Fortified plant milks (calcium + vitamin D).
- Calcium-set tofu, tempeh, legumes.
- Sardines, salmon, mackerel, other fish.
- Low-oxalate leafy greens: kale, collards, bok choy, broccoli, Brussels sprouts, mustard greens.
- Nuts and seeds: almonds, sesame, tahini, chia, flaxseed.
- Whole grains: oats, brown rice, quinoa, barley, whole-grain bread.
- Fruit: berries, citrus, kiwi, figs, prunes, bananas.
- Eggs and poultry.

## Limit or avoid

| Item | Reason |
|---|---|
| Salt, soy sauce, bouillon, pickles, olives, capers | Sodium increases calcium excretion |
| Bacon, ham, sausage, deli meat, processed meat | Sodium + nitrates + low nutrient density |
| Liver, cod liver oil, high-dose retinol supplements | Preformed vitamin A increases fracture risk |
| Cola and phosphoric-acid soft drinks | Associated with lower bone density |
| Energy drinks and excess coffee (>3–4 cups/day) | Caffeine over 400 mg/day |
| Excess alcohol | Impairs bone formation and increases falls |
| Very low-calorie diets | Bone and muscle loss |
| High-dose calcium supplements without medical guidance | Kidney stone and cardiovascular risk |

## Oxalate note

Spinach, rhubarb, beet greens and Swiss chard are high in oxalates, which bind calcium and reduce absorption. They are still healthy — just do not rely on them as the main calcium source. Kale, collards and bok choy are low-oxalate and better calcium contributors.

## Meal patterns

- **Breakfast:** yogurt with berries, oats and almonds; fortified plant milk smoothie with tahini and banana; eggs with kale and whole-grain toast.
- **Lunch:** sardine and tomato salad with olive oil; tofu and broccoli stir-fry with brown rice; lentil soup with kale.
- **Dinner:** salmon with potatoes and collard greens; chicken with quinoa and bok choy; bean and vegetable stew with tahini drizzle.
- **Snacks:** yogurt, almonds, sesame snaps (low sugar), dried figs, prunes, cheese (moderate).

## Couple cooking

- The bone-health partner's plate should include the calcium accent (yogurt, tahini, sardines, tofu) even when the shared dish is built around other ingredients.
- Keep salt out of the shared pan and let the other partner add it at the table.
