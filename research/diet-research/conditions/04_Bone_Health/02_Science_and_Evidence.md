# Bone Health Diet — Science & Evidence

## Calcium

- **RDA:** 1,000 mg/day for men 19–70 and women 19–50; 1,200 mg/day for women 51+ and men 71+ (US Institute of Medicine / National Academies).
- **Food first:** dairy, fortified plant milks, calcium-set tofu, sardines and salmon with bones, low-oxalate leafy greens, almonds and tahini.
- **Absorption:** calcium absorption is most efficient in doses of ≤500 mg at a time; split intake across meals.
- **Supplements:** use only to fill a measured gap; high-dose calcium supplements have been associated with kidney stones and possibly cardiovascular risk. Total intake (food + supplements) above 2,000–2,500 mg/day should be avoided.
- **Trials:** calcium plus vitamin D supplementation produces modest increases in bone mineral density and, in some trials, reductions in fracture risk, particularly in institutionalised older adults.

## Vitamin D

- **RDA:** 600 IU/day for adults 19–70; 800 IU/day for adults 71+; the National Osteoporosis Foundation suggests 800–1,000 IU/day for adults 50+.
- **Mechanism:** vitamin D is required for intestinal calcium absorption and bone mineralisation; deficiency causes osteomalacia and worsens osteoporosis.
- **Sources:** sunlight (main), oily fish, egg yolks, fortified foods. Dietary intake is usually insufficient without fortification.
- **Testing:** 25-hydroxyvitamin D testing guides supplementation; doses >4,000 IU/day require medical supervision.

## Protein

- **Bone matrix** is roughly half protein by volume; adequate protein supports bone formation and muscle mass.
- **Target:** 1.0–1.2 g/kg/day for older adults (higher than the 0.8 g/kg RDA), spread across meals.
- **Trials:** higher protein intake is associated with higher bone mineral density and, in some cohorts, lower fracture risk — provided calcium intake is adequate.
- **Caution:** in advanced kidney disease, protein must be individualised.

## Vitamin K

- Vitamin K1 (phylloquinone) is required for gamma-carboxylation of osteocalcin, a key bone protein.
- Observational studies link higher vitamin K intake with lower hip fracture risk; interventional evidence is mixed.
- **Sources:** kale, collards, spinach, broccoli, Brussels sprouts, parsley.
- **Caution:** people on warfarin need consistent (not necessarily low) vitamin K intake and medical guidance.

## Magnesium, potassium and vitamin C

- **Magnesium:** ~50–60% of body magnesium is in bone; deficiency impairs bone quality. Food sources: nuts, seeds, legumes, whole grains, leafy greens.
- **Potassium:** higher intake is associated with higher bone density, possibly via acid-base balance.
- **Vitamin C:** supports collagen cross-linking in bone matrix; fruit and vegetables are the main sources.

## Sodium, caffeine, alcohol and vitamin A

- **Sodium:** each ~2,300 mg of sodium increases urinary calcium loss by roughly 40–80 mg; high-salt diets are associated with lower bone density and higher fracture risk.
- **Caffeine:** >400 mg/day is associated with a modest reduction in bone density, especially when calcium intake is low.
- **Alcohol:** >2 drinks/day impairs bone formation and increases fall risk; heavy drinking is a major secondary cause of osteoporosis.
- **Retinol (preformed vitamin A):** intakes above ~1,500 µg RAE/day (or >3,000 IU/day) are associated with increased hip fracture risk. Avoid liver and cod liver oil; beta-carotene from plants does not carry the same risk.
- **Cola drinks:** associated with lower bone mineral density, likely through phosphoric acid and displacement of milk.

## Dietary patterns

- **DASH and Mediterranean patterns** are associated with better bone mineral density and lower fracture risk, likely through calcium, potassium, magnesium and protein.
- **Prunes:** 50 g/day for 6–12 months has been associated with preserved bone density in postmenopausal women (moderate evidence).
- **Fruits and vegetables** provide potassium, magnesium, vitamin K and vitamin C, and support acid-base balance.

## Evidence grading

- Calcium + vitamin D for bone health: **Grade A/B** (strong for deficiency correction; modest fracture effect).
- Adequate protein for bone and muscle: **Grade B**.
- Vitamin K for bone: **Grade B** (observational; mixed trials).
- Reducing excess sodium/alcohol: **Grade B**.
- Prunes for bone density: **Grade B** (small trials).
- High-dose retinol harm: **Grade B/C** (observational).
