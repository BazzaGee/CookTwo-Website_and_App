# Low-Sodium / Cardiac-Friendly Diet — Health Outcomes

## Blood pressure

- Sodium reduction lowers blood pressure in a dose-dependent way; the DASH-Sodium trial showed the largest reductions at 1,500 mg/day combined with the DASH pattern.
- Effects appear within 1–4 weeks and are sustained with adherence.
- Greater benefit in older adults, people with hypertension, salt-sensitive individuals and Black populations.

## Cardiovascular risk

- Lower sodium intake is associated with reduced risk of cardiovascular events and stroke in prospective cohorts, especially when combined with a DASH-style pattern.
- Replacing saturated fat with unsaturated fat lowers LDL cholesterol and reduces cardiovascular events.
- Higher fibre intake (25–30 g/day) is associated with lower cardiovascular and all-cause mortality.
- Oily fish intake (2 servings/week) is associated with lower coronary heart disease mortality.

## Heart failure

- Sodium restriction reduces fluid retention and symptoms in heart failure, though the optimal target is debated; 1,500–2,000 mg/day is commonly advised.
- Fluid restriction and diuretic dosing are medical decisions and must not be generated by the app.

## Cholesterol

- Reducing saturated fat to ≤6% of calories and eliminating industrial trans fat lowers LDL.
- Soluble fibre (oats, barley, legumes, psyllium) produces a modest additional LDL reduction.
- Plant sterols/stanols (fortified foods) can further lower LDL but are not a substitute for medication.

## Kidney function

- Sodium restriction reduces blood pressure and proteinuria in chronic kidney disease.
- High-potassium DASH-style patterns require caution in advanced CKD; individualised nephrology guidance takes precedence over this diet.

## Weight and metabolic health

- Low-sodium, high-fibre, minimally processed patterns are associated with lower body weight and improved metabolic markers, largely because they displace ultra-processed foods.
- The diet is not a weight-loss protocol, but it is compatible with calorie-controlled eating.

## Adherence and practical outcomes

- Sodium reduction is difficult in food environments dominated by processed food; label reading and home cooking are the strongest predictors of success.
- Gradual reduction (10–15% at a time) improves palatability and long-term adherence.
- Taste adaptation takes 4–8 weeks; foods that previously tasted normal may taste excessively salty afterwards.

## Caveats

- **Hyponatraemia risk:** excessive fluid intake combined with very low sodium intake can dilute blood sodium, especially in endurance athletes or people on certain medications. "Lower" is not "zero".
- **Kidney disease and potassium:** high-potassium foods may be unsafe in advanced CKD or with potassium-sparing diuretics.
- **Heart failure:** fluid restriction and medication changes are medical decisions only.
- **Medication interactions:** this app does not adjust diuretics, ACE inhibitors, ARBs or statins.

## Evidence grading

- Sodium reduction → lower blood pressure: **Grade A**.
- DASH pattern → lower blood pressure and LDL: **Grade A**.
- Saturated fat reduction → lower LDL: **Grade A**.
- Sodium restriction → fewer cardiovascular events: **Grade B**.
- Heart-failure sodium targets: **Grade B/C** (guideline-based; heterogeneous evidence).
