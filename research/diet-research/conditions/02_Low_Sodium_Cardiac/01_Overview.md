# Low-Sodium / Cardiac-Friendly Diet — Overview

> Health-condition diet for people managing hypertension, elevated cholesterol, heart failure, or cardiovascular risk. Sodium is the primary lever; saturated fat, fibre and unsaturated fats are the secondary levers.

---

## What "low-sodium / cardiac-friendly" means

A dietary pattern that:

1. **Caps sodium** — AHA guidance is **≤2,300 mg/day**, with an ideal target of **≤1,500 mg/day**, especially for people with hypertension.
2. **Limits saturated fat** to ≤5–6% of daily calories and avoids industrial trans fat.
3. **Emphasises** vegetables, fruit, whole grains, legumes, nuts, seeds, fish, lean protein, and unsaturated oils.
4. **Limits** added sugar, refined grains, processed meat, salty snacks and ultra-processed foods.
5. **Encourages potassium-rich foods** (unless kidney disease or potassium-sparing medication makes this unsafe).

This is essentially a strict, condition-oriented application of the DASH and Mediterranean patterns.

## Who it is for

| Condition | Why sodium matters |
|---|---|
| **Hypertension** | Sodium reduction lowers blood pressure; effect is largest in salt-sensitive individuals |
| **Heart failure** | Sodium and sometimes fluid restriction reduce congestion (medical supervision required) |
| **Coronary artery disease / post-MI** | Low saturated fat, high fibre, fish and unsaturated fats support risk reduction |
| **High LDL cholesterol** | Replacing saturated fat with unsaturated fat lowers LDL |
| **Stroke risk** | Blood-pressure control is the dominant mechanism |
| **Chronic kidney disease** | Sodium restriction helps blood pressure and fluid balance, **but potassium may need restriction** — medical supervision required |

## Why sodium is the main target

- About **70% of dietary sodium** comes from packaged, restaurant and processed foods — not the salt shaker.
- Average intake in many Western countries is ~3,400–3,600 mg/day, well above recommendations.
- Reducing sodium lowers blood pressure within weeks; the effect is clinically meaningful even without weight loss.
- Bread, cheese, deli meat, canned soup, sauces and condiments are common "hidden" sources.

## Core principles for meal generation

1. **No added salt** in cooking or at the table; season with herbs, spices, citrus, vinegar, garlic and chilli.
2. **No high-sodium ingredients** — soy sauce, bouillon, stock cubes, pickles, olives, capers, cured/processed meat, salted snacks, canned soup.
3. **Rinse canned legumes** and choose no-salt-added canned goods.
4. **Fresh or frozen plain vegetables** instead of canned with salt.
5. **Unsalted nuts and seeds** only.
6. **Fish twice a week** for omega-3; use lean poultry, legumes and tofu as routine proteins; limit red meat.
7. **Whole grains and fibre** — oats, barley, brown rice, quinoa, whole-grain bread (low-sodium variety).
8. **Unsaturated fats** — olive oil, avocado, nuts, seeds; limit butter, cream and fatty cheese.
9. **Avoid trans fat** — no partially hydrogenated oils, commercial pastries, fried fast food.

## What this diet is not

- Not a licence to remove all fat — unsaturated fats are protective.
- Not a substitute for blood-pressure or cholesterol medication.
- Not appropriate for everyone with kidney disease without individual potassium/phosphorus guidance.
- Not a "zero sodium" diet — the body needs some sodium; the target is reduction, not elimination.
