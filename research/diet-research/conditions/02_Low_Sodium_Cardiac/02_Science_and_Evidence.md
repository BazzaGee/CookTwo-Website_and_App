# Low-Sodium / Cardiac-Friendly Diet — Science & Evidence

## Sodium and blood pressure

- **Dose-response:** randomised trials and meta-analyses show that reducing sodium lowers systolic and diastolic blood pressure in both hypertensive and normotensive people. The effect is larger in older adults, Black populations, and people with salt sensitivity.
- **Magnitude:** a reduction of ~1,000 mg/day is associated with roughly a 2–4 mmHg drop in systolic pressure in hypertensives; greater reductions produce larger effects.
- **Guidelines:** the American Heart Association recommends **≤2,300 mg/day**, moving toward **≤1,500 mg/day** for optimal blood-pressure lowering. The DASH-Sodium trial showed the largest blood-pressure reductions when DASH was combined with **1,500 mg sodium/day**.
- **Sources:** approximately 70% of sodium intake comes from restaurant and packaged foods. Bread, cheese, cured meat, canned soup, sauces and savoury snacks dominate.

## DASH dietary pattern

- DASH (Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension) emphasises vegetables, fruit, whole grains, low-fat dairy, poultry, fish, nuts and legumes, and limits red meat, sweets and saturated fat.
- In controlled feeding trials, DASH lowered systolic blood pressure by ~5–11 mmHg in hypertensives, with the greatest effect when combined with low sodium.
- DASH also lowers LDL cholesterol and is associated with reduced cardiovascular events in cohort studies.

## Saturated fat, trans fat and cholesterol

- **Saturated fat:** the AHA recommends **5–6% of calories** (roughly 11–13 g/day on a 2,000 kcal diet). Each 1% of energy from saturated fat replaced by unsaturated fat is associated with a measurable LDL reduction.
- **Trans fat:** industrial trans fat raises LDL and lowers HDL; the WHO and FDA advise eliminating partially hydrogenated oils. Avoid commercial fried foods, pastries and some margarines.
- **Dietary cholesterol:** less influential than saturated fat for most people, but people with familial hypercholesterolaemia or diabetes may benefit from limiting high-cholesterol foods.
- **Unsaturated fats:** olive oil, avocado, nuts, seeds and oily fish are protective. Replacing saturated fat with polyunsaturated fat reduces cardiovascular events.

## Fibre, potassium and other nutrients

- **Fibre:** 25–30 g/day from whole grains, legumes, vegetables and fruit supports LDL reduction and blood-pressure control.
- **Potassium:** higher potassium intake (3,500–5,000 mg/day from food) lowers blood pressure. **Caution:** potassium restriction may be required in chronic kidney disease or with potassium-sparing diuretics; this must be individualised medically.
- **Magnesium and calcium:** DASH provides ample amounts from vegetables, dairy and nuts; supplements are not routinely needed.
- **Omega-3:** oily fish (salmon, sardines, mackerel) at least twice weekly is associated with lower cardiovascular risk.

## Heart failure

- Sodium restriction (commonly 1,500–2,000 mg/day) is advised to reduce fluid retention, though the evidence for aggressive restriction is mixed.
- Fluid restriction may be required in advanced heart failure — **medical supervision is essential**.
- This app should never set fluid or diuretic targets; it can only generate low-sodium meals.

## Chronic kidney disease caveat

- Sodium restriction is generally beneficial for blood pressure and proteinuria.
- However, DASH-style high-potassium, high-phosphorus patterns can be dangerous in advanced CKD. Users with CKD should follow their nephrologist's individualised plan; this condition must not override that.

## Evidence grading

- Sodium reduction for blood pressure: **Grade A**.
- DASH pattern for blood pressure and cardiovascular risk: **Grade A**.
- Saturated fat reduction for LDL: **Grade A**.
- Fish/omega-3 for cardiovascular risk: **Grade B** (moderate, consistent).
- Heart-failure sodium restriction: **Grade B/C** (guideline-based, heterogeneous trials).
