# Gluten-Free Diet — Overview

> Health-condition diet used when gluten causes harm. This is **not** a lifestyle trend diet for most people: for coeliac disease it is the only treatment, and adherence must be strict and lifelong.

---

## What "gluten-free" means

Gluten is the collective name for the storage proteins (gliadin in wheat, hordein in barley, secalin in rye) that give dough its elasticity. A gluten-free (GF) diet excludes:

- **Wheat** and all its forms — spelt, kamut, farro, emmer, einkorn, durum, semolina, bulgur, couscous, farina, graham.
- **Barley** — including malt, malt extract, malt syrup, malt vinegar and most brewer's yeast.
- **Rye** and **triticale** (a wheat–rye hybrid).

Oats are naturally gluten-free but are almost always cross-contaminated in the field and supply chain; only **certified gluten-free oats** are safe.

## Who needs it

| Condition | Mechanism | Strictness |
|---|---|---|
| **Coeliac disease** (~1% of the population) | Autoimmune reaction to gluten → villous atrophy, malabsorption, long-term cancer and bone risk | Absolute, lifelong, zero intentional gluten |
| **Non-coeliac gluten sensitivity (NCGS)** | Diagnosis of exclusion; symptoms improve on a GF diet | Strict trial then individualised, usually long-term |
| **Wheat allergy** | IgE-mediated allergy to wheat proteins | Avoid wheat; other gluten grains may be tolerated — medical guidance required |
| **Dermatitis herpetiformis** | Skin manifestation of coeliac disease | Absolute, lifelong |

This app encodes the **coeliac-safe standard**, the strictest interpretation. A meal that is safe for coeliac disease is safe for the other groups.

## Regulatory standard (US FDA, 21 CFR 101.91)

- A food labelled "gluten-free" must contain **less than 20 ppm gluten**.
- It may **not** be made with any amount of a gluten-containing grain or a non-processed derivative (e.g. wheat flour, barley malt).
- Hydrolysed and fermented foods (soy sauce, beer, yogurt) have extra record-keeping requirements.
- The label is voluntary — fresh produce, meat and fish may be gluten-free without saying so.

## Core principles for meal generation

1. **No intentional gluten, ever** — including sauces, marinades, stock, coatings and shared equipment.
2. **Whole foods first** — fresh vegetables, fruit, unprocessed meat/fish, eggs, legumes, plain dairy, rice, corn, quinoa, buckwheat, millet, sorghum, teff, amaranth and certified GF oats.
3. **Read every label** — soy sauce, bouillon, sausages, deli meat, imitation crab, seasoned snacks, gravy and some medications commonly hide wheat or barley.
4. **Cross-contact discipline** — separate boards, toasters, fryers and utensils; do not thicken sauces with wheat flour.
5. **Nutritional adequacy** — GF products are often low in fibre, iron, folate and B vitamins. Build meals around naturally GF whole grains and legumes.

## What this diet is not

- Not a weight-loss diet.
- Not appropriate to self-impose without testing — coeliac disease should be diagnosed **before** starting a GF diet, because removing gluten can normalise test results.
- Not a reason to avoid all carbohydrates.
