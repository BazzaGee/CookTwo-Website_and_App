# Gluten-Free Diet — Health Outcomes

## Coeliac disease

- **Symptom resolution:** most people report improved gastrointestinal symptoms within days to weeks of starting a strict GF diet.
- **Mucosal healing:** histological remission is achievable but slow — often 6–24 months; persistent villous atrophy is associated with ongoing gluten exposure (intentional or cross-contact).
- **Nutrient status:** iron, folate, vitamin B12, calcium and vitamin D improve as absorption recovers; deficiencies may need short-term supplementation.
- **Bone health:** untreated coeliac disease is associated with reduced bone mineral density; a GF diet plus adequate calcium/vitamin D helps recovery, though bone density may not fully normalise.
- **Cancer risk:** the increased risk of enteropathy-associated T-cell lymphoma and small-bowel adenocarcinoma declines with sustained adherence, though it may not return fully to population baseline.
- **Quality of life:** adherence improves fatigue, abdominal pain, bloating and general wellbeing; the burden of the diet itself can affect social eating and mental health.

## Non-coeliac gluten sensitivity

- Symptom improvement is common in the short term; blinded trials show heterogeneous and often modest effects.
- A structured rechallenge is important to avoid unnecessary long-term restriction and nutritional gaps.
- FODMAP intolerance (wheat fructans) should be considered before committing to lifelong gluten avoidance.

## Wheat allergy

- Strict wheat avoidance prevents acute reactions; other gluten grains are usually tolerated.
- Cross-reactivity with other grains is uncommon but possible; specialist guidance is required.

## Nutritional adequacy

- Well-planned GF diets can meet all nutrient needs.
- Risks arise when GF diets rely on refined GF substitutes: lower fibre, iron, folate and B vitamins, and higher sugar/fat.
- Protective pattern: naturally GF whole grains, legumes, vegetables, fruit, nuts, seeds, fish and lean protein.

## Special populations

- **Children and adolescents:** growth monitoring and dietitian support are essential; GF diets must be nutritionally complete.
- **Pregnancy:** strict GF adherence protects maternal and fetal outcomes; folate, iron, calcium and vitamin D deserve attention.
- **Type 1 diabetes + coeliac:** common combination; carbohydrate counting must account for GF product composition.
- **Athletes:** GF diets are not performance-enhancing unless coeliac disease or NCGS is present; ensure adequate carbohydrate and iron.

## Monitoring

- Coeliac serology (tTG-IgA) and, where indicated, follow-up biopsy.
- Iron studies, B12, folate, calcium, vitamin D and bone density in at-risk patients.
- Dietitian review and annual adherence check.

## Absolute caveats

- A gluten-free diet is **not** a treatment for autism, thyroid disease, infertility or general fatigue in people without coeliac disease or NCGS.
- The app provides meal guidance, not diagnosis or medical advice. Anyone with suspected coeliac disease should be tested **before** removing gluten.
