# Diabetes-Friendly Diet — Health Outcomes

## Glycaemic control

- Medical nutrition therapy lowers HbA1c by approximately **0.3–1.0%**, with larger effects in people with higher baseline HbA1c and when combined with medication and physical activity.
- Higher fibre, lower glycaemic index and elimination of sugary drinks each contribute independently.
- Weight loss of 5–10% in people with type 2 diabetes and overweight produces clinically meaningful HbA1c reductions.

## Cardiovascular risk

- People with diabetes have roughly twice the cardiovascular risk of the general population; diet is a major modifiable factor.
- Mediterranean-style patterns rich in olive oil, nuts, fish, legumes and vegetables reduce major cardiovascular events in high-risk populations (PREDIMED).
- Replacing saturated fat with unsaturated fat lowers LDL; eliminating trans fat removes a potent risk factor.
- Higher fibre intake is associated with lower cardiovascular and all-cause mortality.

## Weight and metabolic health

- Whole-food, fibre-rich, lower-energy-density patterns support weight loss without hunger.
- Reduced intake of ultra-processed foods and sugary drinks is associated with lower type 2 diabetes incidence.
- Prediabetes: intensive lifestyle intervention (7% weight loss plus activity) reduced progression to diabetes by ~58% in the Diabetes Prevention Program.

## Kidney health

- In diabetic kidney disease, blood-pressure control and sodium reduction are protective.
- Protein intake should be individualised; very-high-protein diets are not recommended in established kidney disease.

## Eye and nerve complications

- Tight glycaemic control reduces the risk of retinopathy, nephropathy and neuropathy, especially in type 1 diabetes (DCCT) and type 2 diabetes (UKPDS).
- Diet is one component; medication adherence, blood pressure and lipid control matter equally.

## Mental health and eating behaviour

- Restrictive diabetes diets can increase guilt and disordered eating. The emphasis should be on sustainable patterns, not perfection.
- Carbohydrate awareness is more effective than carbohydrate fear.

## Caveats

- **Hypoglycaemia:** people on insulin or sulfonylureas may become hypoglycaemic if meals are delayed, skipped or very low in carbohydrate. The app must not promote fasting or meal skipping for these users.
- **Type 1 diabetes:** carbohydrate counting must match insulin therapy; the app does not calculate insulin.
- **Pregnancy and gestational diabetes:** individualised targets and medical supervision are required.
- **Older adults:** malnutrition risk may outweigh tight glycaemic targets; individualise.

## Evidence grading

- MNT → improved HbA1c: **Grade A**.
- Fibre-rich diet → improved glycaemia and lipids: **Grade A/B**.
- Low-GI diet → modest HbA1c improvement: **Grade B**.
- Mediterranean diet → cardiovascular risk reduction: **Grade A**.
- Sugary-drink reduction → lower diabetes risk: **Grade A**.
- Weight loss → glycaemic improvement and possible remission: **Grade A** (remission durability is uncertain).
