# Diabetes-Friendly Diet — Overview

> Health-condition diet for people living with type 2 diabetes, prediabetes, or type 1 diabetes who want meal patterns that support stable blood glucose. It is **not** a treatment protocol and never replaces medication or insulin guidance.

---

## What "diabetes-friendly" means

There is no single "diabetic diet". Evidence-based guidance (ADA Standards of Care) converges on a **whole-food, carbohydrate-aware pattern**:

1. **Carbohydrate quality and consistency** — favour non-starchy vegetables, whole fruit, legumes, whole grains, nuts and seeds; minimise refined grains, added sugars and ultra-processed foods.
2. **Portion awareness** — keep carbohydrate portions consistent meal to meal; the app uses a moderate per-meal ceiling of **60 g carbohydrate** as a default guardrail.
3. **Fibre** — 25–30 g/day (14 g per 1,000 kcal) improves glycaemic control and satiety.
4. **Protein** — lean poultry, fish, eggs, legumes, tofu and plain dairy; protein does not raise glucose sharply.
5. **Fat quality** — unsaturated fats (olive oil, avocado, nuts, oily fish); limit saturated fat.
6. **Added sugar and sugary drinks** — the strongest evidence for harm; eliminate sugary drinks.
7. **Consistent meal rhythm** — regular meals, especially for people on insulin or sulfonylureas.

## Who it is for

| Condition | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Prediabetes** | Weight loss of 5–7% and a whole-food, lower-carb pattern reduce progression to type 2 diabetes |
| **Type 2 diabetes** | MNT improves HbA1c; carbohydrate awareness and fibre are central |
| **Type 1 diabetes** | Carb counting with insulin is individualised — the app can suggest balanced meals but must never calculate insulin doses |
| **Gestational diabetes** | Individualised carbohydrate distribution with a dietitian; medical supervision required |

## Important boundaries

- The app **does not** calculate insulin, adjust medication, or set blood-glucose targets.
- Hypoglycaemia risk exists for people on insulin or sulfonylureas; meal skipping and very-low-carbohydrate meals can be dangerous without medical guidance.
- People with diabetes and chronic kidney disease need individualised protein and potassium advice.
- "Sugar-free" products are not automatically healthy — they may contain refined starch or sugar alcohols that affect digestion.

## Core principles for meal generation

1. **Build the plate:** ½ non-starchy vegetables, ¼ protein, ¼ carbohydrate-rich whole food.
2. **Choose low-glycaemic carbohydrates:** legumes, barley, oats, quinoa, whole-grain bread, sweet potato, whole fruit.
3. **Never add sugar** — no sugary drinks, sweets, desserts, sweetened yogurt, jam or sugary sauces.
4. **Prefer whole fruit over juice**; keep fruit portions moderate.
5. **Include fibre at every meal** — vegetables, legumes, nuts, seeds.
6. **Include protein at every meal** to blunt glucose spikes.
7. **Use unsaturated fats**; limit butter, cream and fatty meat.
8. **Keep carbohydrate portions consistent** — the app targets 30–60 g per main meal.
9. **No alcohol on an empty stomach**; alcohol can cause delayed hypoglycaemia in people on insulin.

## What this diet is not

- Not a zero-carbohydrate diet — very-low-carb patterns require medical and dietitian oversight, especially with diabetes medication.
- Not a replacement for HbA1c monitoring, medication or diabetes education.
- Not a guarantee of glucose control — individual responses vary.
