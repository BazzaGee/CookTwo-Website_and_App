The bone-health diet is the pattern whose evidence runs longest: the trials and cohorts target outcomes years or decades away, and the RDAs are not dietary guessing-games but longitudinal *recommendations from the Institute of Medicine* built on decades of work. It covers calcium, vitamin D, protein, vitamin K, magnesium, potassium, and vitamin C — and trips everyone up by tasting mostly invisible: the reason you do not experience a calcium deficit is *dairy-fetching liberality plus less-processed cooking* plus vitamin D sun habits.

The point everyone forgets: the *invisible* side of bone health is the negative column. Sodium raises urinary calcium loss — each ~2,300 mg/day is associated with 40–80 mg of extra calcium excretion — and cola drinks track with lower bone mineral density. Bone health runs as a 60-year debt-repayment brief: marketing assumes the positive levers are vitamins; the research says exercise plus protein plus minerals contribute, and sodium, excess retinol and cola quietly subtract.

## Building the bone-health plate

1. **Calcium by food first.** Dairy — dairy or fortified alternatives; calcium-set tofu; sardines or canned salmon *with bones*; low-oxalate leafy greens (kale, bok choy, collards) rather than spinach (whose calcium is poorly absorbed); almonds and tahini. Absorption tops out around ≤500 mg per dose — split doses across meals.
2. **Vitamin D — twice.** White-sun and oily fish and egg yolks are the *dietary* sources; SARS-era studies found dietary intake from unfortified sources rarely provides the 800+ IU/day range on a typical Western plate. Testing your 25-OH-D level guides a supplement; doses beyond 4,000 IU/day belong under medical supervision.
3. **Protein— for the bone matrix.** Older adults at 1.0–1.2 g/kg/day: trials show *dense bone structure* correlation and cohort data links high-adequate protein to fracture risk reduction — with calcium adequacy a *precondition*. (If advanced kidney disease is present, this becomes a nephrology-controlled number.)
4. **Vitamin K, magnesium, potassium, vitamin C.** All arrive on the produce-and-legumes plate: kale, broccoli and Brussels sprouts for K1; nuts, seeds and legumes for magnesium; fruit for vitamin C's role in collagen cross-linking.
5. **The negative column.** Each of these been squealing-loud in the research:
   - Sodium above 2,300 mg/day = 40–80 mg extra calcium loss per day.
   - Cola-style drinks: associated with lower bone mineral density, attributable partly to phosphoric acid and partly to their depressive displacement of milk.
   - Alcohol beyond 2 drinks/day: impairs bone formation and raises fall risk.
   - Preformed vitamin A (retinol): above ~1,500 µg RAE/day, *higher*, not lower, hip fracture risk — so no clinical liver-and-cod-liver-oil flux; beta-carotene does not transfer this risk.

## Exercise, the twin lever

The diet and exercise exports — body-resistance and impact motion from walking to dance and strength — put *multiple* bone reads in motion: without load-bearing exercise, bone density's diet ceiling gets treated as medicine's ceiling. The Osteoporosis Association (and national Osteoporosis populations equivalent programmes like NOF) consistently couples: bone mineral density to loading exercise data plus calcium + vitamin D food adequacy — with a segment of the trial record showing calcium-driven BMD increases only under load-bearing arms.
