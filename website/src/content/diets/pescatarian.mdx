Of the five Adventist patterns, one quietly beat them all. In the 2013 JAMA Internal Medicine analysis of 73,308 people, *pesco-vegetarians* — fish allowed, meat not — tracked the lowest all-cause mortality of any pattern on the board (HR 0.81), ahead of vegans (0.85), lacto-ovo vegetarians (0.91) and semi-vegetarians (0.92). The explanation is not mystical: the pattern inherits the plant-forward advantage from its vegetarian half and then solves the two nutrient problems plant-only patterns complain about — B12 and EPA/DHA omega-3 — by leaving fish on the menu.

This guide covers what the cohort evidence, the meta-analyses and the fish-supply research actually show, and how to build the pattern with sport-fish contaminant awareness (sardines, not swordfish) without becoming paranoid.

## What the evidence shows

<StudyCard name="Yamagishi K et al. and related Asian fish cohorts" design="Cohort syntheses" participants="—" duration="—" finding="Fish intake is inversely associated with stroke risk in both Western and Japanese cohorts — with the pattern not only adding fish but replacing red and processed meat, a double displacement." url="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21747204/" note="Review-level; fish's cardiovascular effects run through direct omega-3 plus displacement." />

Two structural points flesh that picture out. First, the fish-eating cohort literature studies fish as part of the pattern — sitting beside produce and legumes, not as a solo intervention against the same. Second, the *species-level* work matters more than the category-level: small oily fish — sardines, mackerel, herring, anchovies — carries high omega-3 with the lowest mercury positions. Large predators — shark, swordfish, king mackerel, bigeye tuna — accumulate mercury over decades. One is a nutrition workhorse; one is a protein you eat a few times a year at most.

<Callout variant="quote" title="DHA and EPA are the reason fish earns the menu">
The plant omega-3s ALA needs conversion to the active EPA and DHA forms at what is, in practice, very low yields — with the conversion slowed further by the replacement of omega-6-rich seed oils. Because fish supplies DHA and EPA ready to use, the pattern's omega-3 health claims are more physically direct than the vegan-chia flexibility that promises the same via supplements. This is a small but real "premium" of the pescatarian structure.
</Callout>

## The two contaminant questions, actually answered

- **Mercury.** Eat small oily fish weekly; save bigeye tuna and swordfish for rare occasions; in pregnancy, follow FDA/EPA fish advisories strictly. The mercury question does not argue against a pescatarian pattern — it argues for the bottom-shelf sardine.
- **Environmental load.** Wild-capture concerns vary by fishery; aquaculture continues to improve in some regions. Either way, canned sardines and mackerel carry low footprints relative to beef or heavily-farmed salmon. A good rule: **swim down the food chain.**
