For diabetes, the "diet" is not a diet. It is **medical nutrition therapy** — a piece of the *treatment* protocol, credentialled and monitored alongside your endocrinology, and graded A for the clinical role it plays: randomised trials and systematic reviews show dietary intervention lowers HbA1c by roughly **0.3–1.0 percentage points**, with the biggest effects delivered by a registered dietitian alongside medication and activity changes.

Underneath that number sits a clear set of food rules. The interesting medical-nutrition point is that there is *no* single optimal macro ratio — the American Diabetes Association deliberately does not prescribe one — and prioritises food quality, energy balance and personalisation. Carbohydrate quality, fibre, portion *consistency* and weight management together do the heavy lifting; GI tools, low-GI selection, and carb counting provide practical scaffolding.

<Callout variant="quote" title="HbA1c, the diagnostic marker, in plain terms">
Haemoglobin A1c measures average blood sugar over ~3 months; a healthy adult runs about 5.0–5.6% — under 5.7% is normal, 5.7–6.4% prediabetes, and 6.5%+ on two checks indicates diabetes. Each +1% in the long-run tracks with higher cardiovascular and microvascular risk. The 5–10% body-weight loss (DiRECT and cooperative trials) sits at a predictable slope: every point of HbA1c reduction, roughly, is a real risk reduction.
</Callout>

## The pillars to know, and the drug-adjustment traps

- **Carb quality over quantity.** The whole-grains/legumes/vegetable primary carb list is where the evidence colours the plate: post-meal glucose reads dramatically differently for 50 g of intact carb versus 50 g of refined. One premium fact deserves flagging: sugar-sweetened drinks are the single strongest dietary driver of new type 2 diabetes, and dropping them is a first-order priority.
- **Fibre, 25–30 g/day minimum.** Its effects are on both * LDL cholesterol* and *glycaemic control* — reviews of soluble-psyllium and oats grade the latter as a small but real HbA1c positive.
- **Carbohydrate consistency for insulin/sulfonylurea users.** Meals skipped out of *strictness* rather than *suspension* is the classic hypoglycaemia trap. Anyone on insulin or sulfonylureas should not drastically change the meal schedule without endocrine and clinician supervision — a real medical caution that both the ADA and any responsible app guidance needs.
- **Have a kidney caveat:** if diabetic kidney disease is present, the protein target belongs under nephrology control.

## Whom this pattern suits, precisely

- All adults living with **type 2 diabetes or prediabetes** — with the *tightest* valuable range on meals that can be maintained long-term.
- Polycystic ovary syndrome and insulin-resistance work — of the ADA-approved MNT options this shares the closest chemistry.
- **Anyone starting glucose medications** — the meal plan gets built with the medication schedule, rather than around it.
