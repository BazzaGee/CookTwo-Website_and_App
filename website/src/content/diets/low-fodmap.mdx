The low FODMAP diet is the one therapeutic pattern on this map with a genuinely clinical track record — developed in Australia by Monash University, tested in randomized controlled feeding trials, and endorsed by gastroenterology guidelines worldwide. FODMAPs — **fermentable oligosaccharides, disaccharides, monosaccharides and polyols** — are short-chain carbohydrates that gut bacteria ferment rapidly and that pull water into the bowel. For a healthy colon this process is quiet and healthy; for a person with IBS it means gas, distension, cramping and urgency.

The evidence is uncommonly clear: roughly **70% of IBS patients get meaningful relief on the elimination phase**, versus about 20% following typical Australian eating. That statistic leads every honest write-up of the diet, and the guide immediately below it is unusual on the map in one critical detail — **the diet is a diagnostic funnel, not a permanent plate**. Run properly, it runs in three phases and lands back at a broad, food-quality-complete intake: just minus the handful of fermentables that do not agree with you.

<Callout variant="tip" title="Why simple advice worked nearly as well in that trial">
NICE-style advice — regular meals, less fat, less caffeine and alcohol, standard fibre — lands IBS relief for a meaningful slice of patients. That is worth remembering before anyone turns a *diagonal elimination* into a lifelong lifestyle. If your symptoms are moderate and you have never tried simple eating-schedule changes first, start there.
</Callout>

## How the three phases actually work

1. **Elimination (2–6 weeks).** Strictly diminish fermentable oligo-, di-, monosaccharides and polyols: fructans (wheat, onion, garlic), lactose, high-fructose fruits, legumes, and sweeteners from sorbitol to xylitol. Symptom relief establishes that fermentables are indeed your trigger — **this is clinical instrumentation, not the goal**.
2. **Systematic reintroduction (6–8 weeks).** One FODMAP group at a time, at graded doses, tracked carefully. Most IBS patients only react to a **subset** of the groups — many tolerate everything except, for instance, the fructan family — which makes long-term eating far less restricted than the elimination list suggests.
3. **Personalised maintenance.** Return to a broadly unrestricted plate built around the specific groups that did and did not trouble you. In practice, *most people only need to moderate two or three groups* — which is a far better long-term landing than the strict elimination menu.

## The honest traps

**The microbiome cost of staying on elimination.** Cumulative evidence says the elimination phase reduces *bifidobacteria* — one of the healthy families that fibre from garlic, onion and legumes feeds. Staying on elimination is harmful *when the phase is supposed to have ended*. If you are still eating the restricted list at a six-month mark for a diet named a "diagnostic", the diet has drifted away from its purpose.

**Weight, calcium, micronutrients.** Long-term restriction of legumes and lactose runs into calcium and protein gaps, and creatine and wheat-avoidance complicates iron — a dietitian's routines check on each, and fortifies where needed.

**IBS-c trigger foods.** Result varies by IBS subtype: constipation-dominant individuals may find the *reduced fibre* further slowing transit; the dietitian may add fluid and fibre else — rather than assuming restriction is uniformly helpful.
