A book in this corner of the map — **high-carb diet** — has two lives. In its healthy form it is the food pattern of the longest-lived population ever recorded: Okinawa, at roughly 85% carbohydrate (sweet potato), 6% fat and 9% protein, ran whole, plant-heavy carbohydrate as *engine* for decades. In its philosophically strict form it is Dr John McDougall's Starch Solution — plant-only, starch-centric, added fat under 10% of calories — pattern graded C-D here for clinical claims because its own published evidence base is small and mostly uncontrolled.

The second template is worth reading closely because it makes a specific, testable claim: that calories are not mastered unless the carb is refined, so whole starches can be eaten freely without weight gain. That claim is backed better than its marketing implies — and this page separates it from the ones it is blended with.

## If you want the healthy version, this is the shape

High-carb eating, done well, looks like this: plant food at 80–90% of the plate; starches as the energy engine — potatoes, sweet potatoes, rice, beans, corn, squash, whole grains; vegetables in volume; fruit daily; little or no refined flour, sugar or packaged "health" products; animal foods rare or absent. The Okinawa evidence, laid out in the [hub’s global pattern map](/diets#map), is the strongest proof of concept that this architecture sustains human health at a population scale — if the carb is whole, the lifestyle includes activity, and the portion total stays reasonable.

What happens when the shape keeps its identity but the fat climbs is visible in [our analysis of the map](/diets#spectrum) — with Mediterranean, DASH or even a plant-forward pescatarian model tending the same grain with olive oil etc., the results plan better.

## The honest traps of this pattern

**Protein slide.** Carb is 12–15% of calories by design; an adult who needs 1.5 g/kg from soy and tempeh overheads. It is makeable — but requires deliberate volume. The pattern's *sloganeering* "unlimited" will not get older-adult protein floors.

**B12 is absent** and miso is not B12 (it carries inactive analogues). This is a vegan-shaped obligation: supplement routinely.

**Fat-soluble vitamins and DHA.** With oil excluded, vitamin E, K drops, absorption of carotenoids from salads — the trial-proven pattern of adding a small amount of fat to plant carotenoids — cuts hard. Algae-oil and a small daily nulled (with or without daily classical supplementation) may be a plan's footprint.

**Socially.** Every shared meal is a negotiation without an oil plate either at the table or in the cooking of it. Family kitchens default to fat for flavour — healthy fats, but fats — and this plan asks the cook to build flavour from acids, heat and aromatics instead. Workable; choose it knowingly.

**McDougall's protocol carries two different claims, and they are not the same.** The no-oil version (under 10% fat) is the strictest mainstream shape, and its clinical evidence arrives through the combined Ornish and Esselstyn programmes — which all bundled exercise, stress management and statins with the diet. The broader claim — "unlimited whole starches" — has partial support from the Okinawan reference data. What the evidence does not support is treating the extreme no-oil version as the only correct shape: Mediterranean, at ~37% fat, carried a 30% cardiovascular-event reduction in PREDIMED. The starches are the engine; the oil ban is negotiable priority, not a law of nature.

## Who it suits

- **Lovers of potatoes, rice and beans by default** — with the same ten dishes rotating weekly, the pattern is simple rather than intricate.
- **Weight-loss seekers who fail on the "eat less" instruction** — whole starch volume produces spontaneous saturation; this is the pattern classically aligned with people who feel hungry on keto.
- **Households cooking plant-based anyway** — one shared base of starch and vegetables, with duelling plates solved at the pantry. Cross-references live in [the plant-forward section](/diets#catalog).
