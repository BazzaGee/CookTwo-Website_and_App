80/10/10 — advertised as 80% carbohydrate, 10% protein, 10% fat from raw, fruit-anchored plant food — is the most extreme *and* the least-evidenced pattern on this map, and it earns that double superlative in the same breath. Like carnivore on the opposite corner, it grades D: no randomized trials, no cohorts, no comparative research. Its primary promotion is one book, and its primary proof is testimonial plus assertion about humanity's primate past.

What exists around the edges is genuinely useful for the wider picture, though — because raw-food research of comparable shape (not the 80/10/10 brand itself, but similar raw vegan protocols studied in cohorts like the Giessen Raw Food Study) has actually been measured. Those findings — very low LDL, very low HDL, elevated homocysteine, amenorrhea in 30% of women under 45, and measurable bone loss — describe exactly the physiological worries that the marketing waves should mention and does not. This page pulls them into one guide.

## What the pattern is about

Direct source: Douglas Graham's "The 80/10/10 Diet" (2006). The claim lattice: humans are naturally frugivores; fat above 10% causes insulin resistance; cooked food is denatured; protein in excess (above 10% of total calories) is toxic and calcium-leaching. The proposed solutions: fruit for the bulk of calories (bananas, mangoes, dates, melons), greens and celery in large volume, no cooked foods, no grains, no legumes, no oils, no salt. Sweet-vegan, near-pure-carb, unmodified.

Both the fat claim and the protein claim are *half*-truths. Saturated and processed fats hold up poorly; olive oil, nuts, avocados and fish omega-3s do the opposite in the cohort data. The "we're frugivores" argument collapses on the actual ethnography of traditional populations — this hub's global map includes Arctic Inuit populations on 70–80% fat and Okinawans on 85% carbohydrate, with no evidence either population cooked poorly or degraded their health.

Extremes look healthy on lab panels whose reference ranges are built around average people. LDL can spot-check well while HDL fell below safe levels and homocysteine climbed; "blood values look great" in the west is usually a *screening-tool interpretation* performed without a clinical review. A 34-page Instagram story about blood work is not the same as a metabolic panel reviewed by a clinician.

## The honest deficits

- **Protein: 10% of calories meets neither the WHO-mandated minimum nor the practical needs of adults doing sport, aging, pregnancy, healing, or losing weight.** So the alleged "10% enough" claim assumes the pattern's *strict, individual*, and utterly atypical bodily detail.
- **B12 is absent.** Fermented-sources claim — common in the raw community — does not hold an active source. Supplements are the only real structure.
- **Fat beyond DHA** — vitamin A, E, K absorption and DHA availability both drop without fish or algae-derived EPA/DHA.
- **Minerals — calcium, iron, zinc and iodine need care.** What the "natural frugivore" argument forgets is that our closest primate cousins did not swim in modern supermarket produce.

## Who should never run this

- Pregnancy, breastfeeding, or menstrual-cycle transitions (amenorrhea from low energy availability is documented)
- Adolescence or any growing child
- People with prediabetes or reactive hypoglycaemia — large fruit loads, on a glucose-monitoring subset, produce documented swings
- Anyone with prior eating-disorder history, anyone medically fragile, and anyone with a fitness-training performance target
