Every historical heart-diet recommendation — low sodium, or follow the DASH-shape week — is an *A-grade* in the scientific literature because the trials are unusually decisive: salt-reduction trials show dose-responsive blood-pressure falls; the DASH pattern shows event-reduction in cohort follow-ups. The interesting twist of this pattern is not sodium itself — it is *where the sodium comes from*: roughly **70% of dietary sodium** arises from restaurant meals, packaged food, bread, cheese, cured meats and canned soup rather than from the salt you cook or shake at home. That single fact rewrites the whole pattern: a person who strips most in-packaged and restaurant food off their weekly list has accomplished 80% of the *dietary* side of the cardiac plan — the salt shaker being downstream of the same reality.

On top of sodium, the cardiac plate keeps three structural fundamentals: saturated fat per scope and trans-fat avoidance, unsaturated fat *replacement priority*, and fibre with fish — each documented separately (see the guide's summary below).

## The sodium reality, quantified

<StudyCard name="Sodium reduction dose-response meta-analyses" design="Randomized trials and pooled analyses" participants="—" duration="—" finding="Reducing sodium by roughly 1,000 mg/day drops systolic blood pressure about 2–4 mmHg in hypertensive adults — the effect scales, with larger reductions producing larger drops toward the 1,500 mg/day target. The effect is strongest in older adults, Black populations and people with salt-sensitive physiology." url="https://doi.org/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001053" />

<StudyCard name="The DASH-Sodium trial (NEJM, 2001)" design="Controlled feeding trial, factorial design" participants="412 adults" duration="30 days per condition" finding="Combining the DASH diet with 1,500 mg/day sodium produced the largest documented blood-pressure reduction — up to 8.9 mmHg in hypertensive participants — comparable to single-drug therapy. Diet quality amplifies sodium discipline." url="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11136953/" />

Guideline builds: the American Heart Association keeps **≤2,300 mg/day** as the population recommendation — one teaspoon of all dietary salt — and steers toward **1,500 mg/day** for people with high blood pressure where that level is clinically appropriate. The molecular arithmetic is not controversial; the *implementation*, where in your day the sodium actually arrives, is the nontrivial part.

<Callout variant="tip" title="The 8-item list that cuts 70% of the sodium">
Canned soups and broths (choose no-salt-added), cured meats like bacon, ham, salami and hot-dogs; cheese in volume; bread from supermarkets stacked to 300–500 mg/slice; sauces and dressings; canned vegetables without rinsing; salted nuts and crisps; restaurant stews, curries and pho bases. Replace them and the sodium total collapses *without* a single kitchen salt change — because a home-cooked plate of roasted vegetables, herbs-and-lemon chicken, quinoa and a homemade salsa runs only 300–500 mg naturally.
</Callout>

## What to know about sodium and potassium balance

- **Potassium is the counterweight** and its food source is produce, not supplements: 90% of the sodium and potassium conditions in the bloodstream *together* decide blood pressure. This is why the DASH and Mediterranean food specifications align so neatly with sodium discipline: vegetables, legumes, fruit, potatoes deliver the potassium.
- **Salt "alternatives"** — potassium-chloride blends — have their place, but ambivalently, in kidney-disease and drug-interaction territory. Talk it through with a clinician before replacing the dear old salt with **lite salt** at high volume.
- **Iodine awareness:** the natural sodium restriction also reduces iodised salt volume; in pregnancy and iodine-scarce regions, this is worth checking with a dietitian.

## Who this pattern actually fits

- **People with phase-1 blood-pressure elevation** seeking a self-directed intervention with hard *A-grade* outcome data.
- **Cardiovascular-adjacent households** — the pattern is *cooking-first* and pairs naturally with shared-kitchen planning: herbed home-roasted proteins, vegetable-forward bases and sauce construction with acid and heat.
- **Heart-failure tracking** — where sodium restriction has *guideline basis* but heterogeneous trial support, and every target moves under specialist oversight.

Not for anyone managing **advanced chronic kidney disease** without a nephrologist — DASH-style high-potassium patterns are famously tricky at that stage. The same caution applies to heart-failure diuretic schedules, where fluid and medication targets shift with diet changes.
