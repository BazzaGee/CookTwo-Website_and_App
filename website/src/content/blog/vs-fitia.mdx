# CookTwo vs Fitia: Social Fitness Scale vs Couples-First Nutrition

Fitia has grown to 10 million users by making fitness social. Workout challenges, friend leaderboards, community recipes — the app leans into the energy of a crowd. CookTwo takes the opposite approach: couples-first nutrition with no social feed, no public leaderboard, no community pressure. Just the two of you and your food.

![Two couples in a kitchen — one looking at a phone with social fitness content, the other plating different portions from the same dish](/images/blog/vs-fitia-1.webp)

The difference is not that one is better than the other. It is that they solve different problems. Fitia solves motivation through community. CookTwo solves coordination through intimacy.

## What Fitia does well

Fitia's social layer is genuinely engaging. The workout challenges keep people consistent. The friend system adds accountability. The community recipes give ideas that other people have tested and liked.

For individuals who thrive on social motivation, Fitia is a strong tool. The nutrition tracking integrates with the workout side, so you can see how your meals support your training. The 10 million user base means the app has been tested at scale.

The [American College of Sports Medicine](https://www.acsm.org/) recommends at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity exercise per week for adults, and Fitia challenge system is designed to help users reach that target through social accountability. The social features — streaks, badges, friend comparisons — tap into well-documented behavioural psychology principles around habit formation and peer influence.

The community recipes are another strength. Users can submit their own creations, and the most popular ones rise to the top. For individuals who want inspiration from people who are actually doing the work, the community aspect is genuinely valuable. Fitia has successfully turned nutrition tracking into a social activity.

The app also offers a wide range of workout styles — from HIIT to yoga to strength training — all with video demonstrations. The social aspect extends to workouts too: you can see what your friends are doing and join in. For fitness enthusiasts who want community alongside their nutrition tracking, this is a compelling combination. The gamification layer — streaks, badges, level-ups — keeps people engaged longer than apps that rely on willpower alone.

## Where Fitia stops

Fitia's architecture is master/slave — one account creates the plan, the other follows. There is no real-time sync. There is no shared grocery list. There is no adaptive cooking for different targets. There is no couples-first kitchen experience.

For couples, this means one partner typically runs the account and the other is a guest. The mental load falls on the person who sets up the plan. The other partner does not see real-time updates. The grocery list is not shared. The portions are not adapted.

The architecture that works for social fitness — a single user broadcasting to followers — breaks down in a shared kitchen. Fitia was not built for two people making decisions together. The OnePoll survey that couples argue 156 times a year about dinner is not solved by a tool that treats nutrition as an individual pursuit.

The financial cost is also a factor. Two separate accounts for two separate plans means paying twice for the same service. The average couple using individual fitness apps spends $120 per year on subscriptions that do not coordinate. A shared food system eliminates that redundancy.

The practical impact is that couples end up managing two separate systems. One partner tracks their workouts and nutrition in Fitia. The other partner either joins and gets a watered-down experience, or does their own thing separately. The shared kitchen is not where Fitia adds value.

CookTwo starts with the assumption that two people are making food decisions together. Both partners see the same plan. Both partners see the same grocery list. Both partners see the same pantry. The cooking is shared. The plating diverges.

The [one-meal-two-plates](/one-meal-two-plates) method is the core: one recipe, two plates, different macros. The [macro maths](/blog/one-meal-two-macros) are handled automatically. The shared grocery list syncs in under 100 milliseconds. The pantry tracks what you already have so you are not buying duplicates.
